# PLAN 0.29.1 — Adopt `vv-graph` + `sqlite-sparql` upstreams; substrate cuts over to the gem

> *V0.29.x opener. Two upstream projects — [`sqlite-sparql`](https://github.com/laquereric/sqlite-sparql) (Oxigraph-via-sqlite-loadable-rs SQLite extension; `sparql_query` / `sparql_ask` / `sparql_construct` / `rdf_triples` virtual table) and [`vv-graph`](https://github.com/laquereric/vv-graph) (the Rails-ecosystem layer wrapping the extension as ActiveRecord idioms) — are now developed **in parallel to this repo**, not inside it. The MagenticMarket substrate is a **consumer**: it pins both upstreams, wires them into `Gemfile` + `config/database.yml`, and ships the cutover off the in-tree `Triple` AR model + `ProductTripler` to `Semantica::Storable`. Surface refinements feed upstream as PRs, not as in-substrate monkey-patches. No back-compat priority: substrate-side `Triple` / `ProductTripler` are deleted wholesale in the cutover commit. Citations: [`../research/Semantica.md`](../research/Semantica.md) (architectural concept), [`../research/RailsRustSqlite.md`](../research/RailsRustSqlite.md) (technical bridge), [`sqlite-sparql` upstream README](https://github.com/laquereric/sqlite-sparql#readme) (engine surface), [`vv-graph` upstream README](https://github.com/laquereric/vv-graph#readme) (Ruby/Rails surface), [`PLAN_0_28_10.md`](PLAN_0_28_10.md) (the priority queue this slots into). The substrate may keep submodule checkouts under `vendor/{sqlite-sparql,vv-graph}/` for local development convenience, but those checkouts are mount-points for the upstreams — not where the gem/extension is authored.*

## The strategic frame

Three observations land at the same point:

1. **The engine is upstream.** `sqlite-sparql` ships as its own Rust project — Oxigraph wrapped as a SQLite loadable extension. The substrate consumes the compiled `.dylib` / `.so`; it does not author the engine.

2. **The Rails-ecosystem layer is also upstream.** `vv-graph` ships as its own gem — `Loader` / `Sparql` / `Storable` over the extension's raw SQL surface. The substrate consumes the gem path-sourced (during local dev via submodule) or git-sourced (pinned SHA in `Gemfile.lock`); it does not author the gem in-tree.

3. **The substrate is the upstreams' first production consumer.** MagenticMarket has the workload (the product catalog's Schema.org-shaped projection) that exercises the gem's surface. Refinements discovered during the substrate's cutover feed back to upstream as PRs against `vv-graph` / `sqlite-sparql`, not as in-substrate patches.

The strategic move: **pin upstreams, cut over the substrate, push refinements back.** The boundary between this repo and the two upstreams stays bright — substrate consumes; upstreams evolve; surface feedback travels by PR.

## Why no back-compat priority (load-bearing decision)

Standard migration playbooks lean on parity audits, version-pinned shims, deprecation cycles, and "old + new coexist for a release window." None of that applies here:

- **The substrate's `Triple` AR model has one consumer — the substrate itself.** Wholesale deletion in the cutover commit is the discipline; no deprecation surface to honor.
- **`vv-graph` is at v0.x.x upstream.** Until the substrate's needs settle, upstream's surface stays operator-fluid. Standard semver discipline kicks in at v1.0.
- **Coexistence machinery costs more than the migration.** A parity audit comparing old-AR-shape to gem-shape ties two surfaces that have no business being tied. The clean cutover IS the discipline.
- **The substrate pins a specific gem rev.** If upstream drifts mid-migration, the substrate's `Gemfile.lock` stays on the pinned SHA until the substrate is ready to absorb the change. Drift surfaces are resolved by upstream PR, not in-substrate shims.
- **Substrate's commit history is the rollback path.** If the cutover breaks something the test suite missed, `git revert <cutover-commit>` undoes the day's work. No in-codebase version-switch.

Operator response if mid-migration something blocks: stop the cutover commit at the last green point + ship a follow-up. Don't ship a half-migrated state.

## What ships in PLAN_0_29_1 (substrate-side scope only)

### Phase A — Pin upstreams + wire `Gemfile` / `database.yml`

The substrate's `Gemfile` adds the gem with a pinned source (path during local dev, git SHA in CI):

```ruby
# Gemfile — local dev (submodule checkout under vendor/)
gem "vv-graph", path: "vendor/vv-graph"

# Or — pinned SHA (production / CI)
# gem "vv-graph", git: "https://github.com/laquereric/vv-graph",
#                       ref: "<sha>"
```

`config/database.yml` gains the `extensions:` key pointing at the operator-built `libsqlite_sparql.{dylib,so}` (env-var indirected — `MM_SQLITE_SPARQL_PATH` — per the upstream README's quickstart).

Exit: `bundle install` succeeds; `rails runner 'puts Semantica::VERSION'` prints; `rails runner 'puts Semantica::Sparql.ask("ASK { ?s ?p ?o }").inspect'` returns a structured envelope.

### Phase B — Substrate cutover (single commit)

The substrate's existing `Triple` AR model + `ProductTripler` are deleted; replaced by `Product include Semantica::Storable`. Specific changes:

- `app/models/triple.rb` — **deleted.**
- `app/services/product_tripler.rb` — **deleted.**
- `db/migrate/<existing_create_triples>.rb` — keeps the AR table for the data migration to read from; new migration `drop_table :triples` ships at the end of the cutover commit.
- `app/models/product.rb` — gains `include Semantica::Storable` + the `triples do ... end` block matching ProductTripler's existing logic.
- `app/services/wiki_compiler.rb` — `Triple.where(...)` calls replaced with `Semantica::Sparql.select(...)`.
- `lib/tasks/feed.rake` (or wherever `ProductTripler.index_all!` was called) — replaced with `Product.find_each(&:emit_triples!)` (an instance method the gem provides).
- `spec/models/triple_spec.rb` + `spec/services/product_tripler_spec.rb` — **deleted** (gem owns the spec surface; substrate-specific spec moves to product_spec).
- New data migration: walks existing `triples` table rows + calls `Semantica::Sparql.execute('INSERT DATA { ... }')` to populate the new store; then `drop_table :triples` at the end.

### Phase C — `OntologyResolver` Tier 0 (graph traversal)

The existing Tier 1 (stem) / Tier 2 (fuzzy) / Tier 3 (LLM-fallback) cascade gets a new Tier 0: graph-traversal via SPARQL. Given a natural-language category like "wireless printer," Tier 0 walks one-hop neighborhoods in the triple store looking for matching categories.

```ruby
def resolve_via_graph(text)
  results = Semantica::Sparql.select(<<~SPARQL)
    SELECT ?cat WHERE {
      ?p <schema:category> ?cat .
      FILTER(CONTAINS(LCASE(?cat), "#{text.downcase}"))
    } LIMIT 1
  SPARQL
  results[:results].first&.dig("cat")
end
```

Cheaper than the LLM fallback for common cases; finer-grained than fuzzy match. Slots in above Tier 1 (so the cascade becomes Tier 0 / 1 / 2 / 3); audit-locked.

### Phase D — Audits + spec (substrate-side)

- `server/spec/architecture/semantica_substrate_audit_spec.rb` — substrate-side audit: `Product` includes `Semantica::Storable`; legacy `Triple` constant is removed (`expect { Triple }.to raise_error(NameError)`); the `OntologyResolver` cascade has Tier 0 wired; `Gemfile` pins `vv-graph`; `config/database.yml` carries the `extensions:` key.
- `server/spec/services/ontology_resolver_spec.rb` — Tier 0 traversal returns the expected category for fixture inputs; falls through to Tier 1 when no graph match.
- `server/spec/integration/semantica_roundtrip_spec.rb` — end-to-end: create a `Product`, query it back via `Semantica::Sparql.select(...)`, destroy it, verify retraction. Verifies the upstream-gem ↔ substrate contract.

Upstream's own spec suite (Loader / Sparql / Storable surfaces) lives in the `vv-graph` repo; substrate-side specs assume that suite is green at the pinned rev. If a substrate spec catches a gap upstream's specs missed, the fix lands as an upstream PR + a new upstream spec, then the substrate's pin bumps.

### Phase E — Documentation (substrate-side)

- `docs/architecture/Semantica.md` (new — promotion from research/) — moves the elaboration out of research/ since the architecture is now load-bearing. Names the two upstreams + the substrate's consumer relationship. Updates the gap-closing plan: Gap 4 (SPARQL) is closed by PLAN_0_29_1; the other three gaps stay open as PLAN_0_29_2+ work.
- `docs/plans/PLAN_0_28_10.md` — top-of-file callout: "PLAN_0_29_1 opens the V0.29.x arc; substrate consumes `vv-graph` + `sqlite-sparql` upstreams; Tier 3 (Android shell) + Tier 4 deferred items unchanged."
- `README.md` / `QuickStart_Developer.md` — note the new external dependency + the build step for `sqlite-sparql` + the env var (`MM_SQLITE_SPARQL_PATH`).
- Upstream `CHANGELOG.md` entries (in the `vv-graph` + `sqlite-sparql` repos) naming MagenticMarket as first production consumer — authored as upstream PRs, not in this repo.

## What does NOT ship in PLAN_0_29_1

- **Upstream-authored work (gem skeleton, Loader, Sparql facade, Storable concern).** Those belong to the [`vv-graph`](https://github.com/laquereric/vv-graph) repo's own roadmap; this plan only consumes the pinned rev.
- **Engine work (Oxigraph version bumps, vtab features, named graph support).** Belongs to [`sqlite-sparql`](https://github.com/laquereric/sqlite-sparql) upstream.
- **Vector ANN (sqlite-vec + neighbor).** That closes Semantica.md's Gap 1; lands in PLAN_0_29_2.
- **Consolidation subagent (`Mm::Semantica::Consolidator`).** Gap 2; PLAN_0_29_3.
- **Episodic timeline (`Mm::Episodes::Recorder`).** Gap 3; PLAN_0_29_4.
- **Publishing to rubygems.org.** Upstream-side milestone for `vv-graph` at v1.0; not in MM's scope.
- **Cross-machine cache coherence.** The triple store is still per-substrate-process (or per-SQLite-file). Multi-substrate SPARQL is V0.28.x carry-forward (cross-machine sync sub-plan).
- **SPARQL UPDATE write surface for AI Apps.** Upstream's `Semantica::Sparql` facade exposes select / ask / construct; UPDATE is intentionally not in the substrate's Mm::Tool surface (AI Apps that need to mutate the store go through Mm::Tool subclasses that wrap the gem, with the V0.28.10 confirm_per_call gate where appropriate).
- **Operator chrome for the triple store.** Sub-plan after the substrate-side consumption settles; PLAN_0_29.x once operator demand surfaces.

## Order of operations

Upstream work (in the `vv-graph` / `sqlite-sparql` repos — already in flight, NOT scoped by this plan):

- `sqlite-sparql` builds + ships `libsqlite_sparql.{dylib,so}`.
- `vv-graph` ships `Loader` / `Sparql` / `Storable` surfaces with passing specs.

Substrate work (this plan):

1. **Phase A** — pin upstreams + wire `Gemfile` + `config/database.yml`. Exit: `bundle install` succeeds; `Semantica::Sparql.ask(...)` returns an envelope.
2. **Phase B** — substrate cutover commit. Single commit deletes Triple/ProductTripler, adds `Storable` to `Product`, ships the data migration, drops the legacy table.
3. **Phase C** — `OntologyResolver` Tier 0 wiring + spec.
4. **Phase D** — substrate-side audit suite.
5. **Phase E** — docs (Semantica.md promotion + PLAN_0_28_10 callout + QuickStart_Developer.md build-step note; upstream CHANGELOG entries authored as upstream PRs).

Phase B is the irreversible cutover (reversible via `git revert` — but no in-code rollback). Phases C-E are substrate-side polish.

## Exit criteria

PLAN_0_29_1 is shippable when:

- Substrate `Gemfile.lock` pins both `vv-graph` + the matching `sqlite-sparql` build (path or git SHA); `bundle install` clean.
- The substrate's `Triple` constant no longer exists + audit asserts the absence.
- `Product` includes `Semantica::Storable`; a fresh `Product.create!` writes triples to the sqlite-sparql store + the equivalent SPARQL query returns the expected results.
- `WikiCompiler` runs against the new store + produces semantically-equivalent output to its pre-cutover behaviour.
- `OntologyResolver.resolve("printer")` returns the expected category via Tier 0; falls through cleanly for unknown text.
- `bin/mm-smoke` extends with one step probing `Semantica::Loader.ensure_extension_loaded!` + a tiny SPARQL round-trip; goes 8/8 green.
- `QuickStart_Developer.md` build step (operator clones the `sqlite-sparql` submodule, runs `cargo build --release`, sets `MM_SQLITE_SPARQL_PATH`) works copy-paste on a fresh checkout.

Upstream-side gating (assumed green before substrate Phase A starts, not part of this plan's exit):

- `vv-graph`'s own spec suite green at the pinned rev.
- `sqlite-sparql`'s `cargo test` green at the pinned rev.

## What this plan DOES NOT claim

- **Not "MM authors `vv-graph`."** MM is the upstream's first production consumer; refinements travel as upstream PRs, not in-substrate patches. The gem is its own project.
- **Not "the gem is production-ready at v0.1.0."** Upstream's v0.x.x phase is for the duration of substrate-mutual evolution. Outside Rails apps can consume the upstream at their own risk; upstream's README names the v1.0 contract that's NOT yet committed.
- **Not "SPARQL replaces ActiveRecord for product queries."** AR stays the primary query surface for relational lookups. SPARQL slots in where graph-traversal is natural (multi-hop, predicate-driven, semantic-similarity-flavored). Operators mix-and-match.
- **Not "all of Semantica.md's gaps close in PLAN_0_29_1."** Only Gap 4. Gaps 1/2/3 each take their own sub-plan; PLAN_0_29_1 sets the foundation those sub-plans build on.
- **Not "back-compat is unimportant in general."** Back-compat matters when consumers exist; the upstream gem at v0.1.0 has one production consumer (this substrate). v1.0+ inherits standard semver discipline. The `CONSUMER_REQUIREMENT_MM.md` files in both upstream repos make MM's consumer expectations explicit so drift is detectable.

## V0.29.1 status

- **Form**: substrate-side consumer plan against two parallel upstream projects; opens the V0.29.x arc.
- **Action**: Phases A-E per the order of operations. Single substrate cutover commit (Phase B) is the irreversible step; everything before + after is reversible / additive.
- **Risk if ignored**: medium. The substrate ships fine today against the AR `Triple` model; consuming the upstreams is an investment in (a) the substrate's semantic-tier ergonomics, (b) the Rails-ecosystem layer the field is missing — now externalised — and (c) the foundation Gaps 1-3 need. Deferring sits the substrate at "we have triples in SQL" + delays Semantica.md's full close.
- **Trigger for revisit**: Phase B's cutover commit lands cleanly + Phase D's audits pass. If either struggles, hold the substrate pin at the older upstream rev + open an upstream PR for the missing surface before re-attempting the cutover.
