== QUESTION 1 — how is an UNACCEPTED MACHINE SUGGESTION shown? ==

Your design said a suggested card renders with a dashed or double border plus a "Suggested — unaccepted" label. `dashed` is NOT in VARIANTS and VARIANTS is closed. The current build uses variant `quiet` plus a StatusBadge reading "Suggested — unaccepted", and suggestions correctly render NO eligibility band.

Why this is not cosmetic. The board's governance rests on ONE property: a machine proposal must never pass for an established meaning. That property currently holds semantically. Visually it is carried by a badge and a muted treatment — and `quiet` conventionally reads as "less important", not as "not accepted". A decision maker skimming a column could plausibly read a quiet card as a minor accepted meaning. If that happens the governance guarantee is intact in the data and broken in the room.

Choose ONE and justify:
  A. Keep `quiet` + badge. Argue why the badge is sufficient load-bearing signal, and specify exactly what the badge must say and where it must sit so it cannot be missed.
  B. Propose adding a `suggested` variant to the closed VARIANTS set. This is a catalog change: give the numbered proposal, the blast radius (validators, renderer, token set, every existing tree, conformance snapshots, the component library), and say why nothing already in the set will do.
  C. Something else expressible in the CURRENT vocabulary — for example a distinct wrapper kind, a ContextBanner scoping the suggestion region, or a structural placement rule (suggestions grouped in their own DataList under an explicit heading rather than interleaved with accepted cards).

Consider seriously whether SEPARATION beats STYLING here: an interleaved-but-styled suggestion depends on the reader noticing a treatment, whereas a separately grouped one cannot be misread even if styling fails or the library is absent. Note the library MUST degrade — with no include, a page still renders. Does your recommendation survive that?

== QUESTION 2 — what carries the FIVE EXPLORE MARKS? ==

Clicking `explore` on a card marks other cards across columns as one of FIVE states:
  selected · related · likely-hit · suggested · out-of-scope

Your design assigned this to ReferentBridge. That was rejected on build, correctly: ReferentBridge is a six-field REFERENT-RETENTION CLAIM (sourceConcept, sourceDefinitionRevision, targetExpression, mappingArtifact, mappingProof, sourceToTargetScope) and missing any field refuses. Marking a card "likely-hit" is not a retention claim. The implementer could have smuggled a relationClass into the open valueJson — it would have VALIDATED — and refused, because it bends the kind.

The current proposal is StatusBadge + variant. The problem: the usable emphasis-bearing variants are essentially `emphasis` and `quiet`. FIVE marks, roughly TWO variants.

Decide what carries the five marks. Options, argue your own:
  A. StatusBadge per card whose TEXT names the mark, variant used only for weight. Cheap and needs nothing new — but every card in every column gains a badge during exploration, which is visually loud and competes with the eligibility badge already present.
  B. Collapse five marks into fewer distinguishable states. Say WHICH collapse preserves the decision-making value: is `related` vs `likely-hit` a distinction a decision maker acts on differently, or is it a machine-confidence detail that should not drive a visual? `out-of-scope` may be absence-of-mark rather than a mark.
  C. Propose an extension, numbered, with blast radius — a new variant, or the `presentationState` field, or something else. Say why A and B are insufficient.

Constraints that bind either answer: the marks are REQUEST-TIME projection output, never stored; `explore` is `inspect` and must write no relation and mutate no record; a mark must never be confusable with an eligibility band (green/red) or with the suggestion treatment from Question 1 — three different signals may appear on one card at once, and that collision is the hard part.
